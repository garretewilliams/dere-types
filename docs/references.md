# References

Sources for the terminology, language notes and AI character guidance in this repository, grouped by type. First-party writing by the maintainer is listed separately under [Background essays](#background-essays) and is **not** independent evidence.

If you find an error or know a better source, please open a *Factual correction* issue.

## Terminology references

Sources that document how dere terms and related character archetypes are used.

- **Wikipedia: [Tsundere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsundere), [Yandere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yandere), [Kuudere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuudere).** Overviews of the most established terms, with etymology and cited sources. Useful starting points; check their citations for primary sources.
- Galbraith, P. W. (2009). ***The Otaku Encyclopedia: An Insider's Guide to the Subculture of Cool Japan.*** Kodansha International. A reference work on otaku terminology, including character-type vocabulary.
- Galbraith, P. W. (2014). ***The Moé Manifesto: An Insider's Look at the Worlds of Manga, Anime, and Gaming.*** Tuttle Publishing. Interviews and essays on *moe* and character appeal, the context in which dere types developed.
- Azuma, H. (2009). ***Otaku: Japan's Database Animals*** (J. E. Abel & S. Kono, Trans.). University of Minnesota Press. (Original work published 2001.) Describes how fans and creators treat characters as combinations of recognizable "moe elements", a useful frame for understanding archetypes like dere types as reusable components.

Terms not covered by these sources (oujidere, sadodere, mayadere, bakadere, and to some extent himedere and kamidere) are fandom terminology. Their pages describe common usage and mark the confidence of each etymology explicitly.

## Cultural and language references

- **[JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/jmdict/j_jmdict.html)**, Electronic Dictionary Research and Development Group (EDRDG). An open Japanese–multilingual dictionary used here for the meanings of component words such as *hime* (姫, princess), *ōji* (王子, prince), *kami* (神, god), *baka* (馬鹿, fool) and the ideophones *tsun tsun* and *dere dere*. Searchable through [Jisho.org](https://jisho.org/), which is built on JMdict.

Translations of component words describe their ordinary meanings. The meaning of a dere type as a whole comes from fandom usage, not from the dictionary meaning of its parts.

## AI character research

Research on AI characters, role-play with language models, character consistency, long-term interaction and personalization.

- Shanahan, M., McDonell, K., & Reynolds, L. (2023). **Role play with large language models.** *Nature, 623*, 493–498. [doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06647-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06647-8). A framework for understanding LLM dialogue agents as role-playing characters.
- Chen, J., Wang, X., Xu, R., et al. (2024). **From Persona to Personalization: A Survey on Role-Playing Language Agents.** arXiv:2404.18231. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18231).
- Zhang, S., Dinan, E., Urbanek, J., Szlam, A., Kiela, D., & Weston, J. (2018). **Personalizing Dialogue Agents: I have a dog, do you have pets too?** *Proceedings of ACL 2018.* arXiv:1801.07243. The PersonaChat dataset for persona-grounded dialogue.
- Shao, Y., Li, L., Dai, J., & Qiu, X. (2023). **Character-LLM: A Trainable Agent for Role-Playing.** *Proceedings of EMNLP 2023.* arXiv:2310.10158.
- Wang, Z. M., Peng, Z., Que, H., et al. (2023). **RoleLLM: Benchmarking, Eliciting, and Enhancing Role-Playing Abilities of Large Language Models.** arXiv:2310.00746.
- Wang, X., Xiao, Y., Huang, J., et al. (2024). **InCharacter: Evaluating Personality Fidelity in Role-Playing Agents through Psychological Interviews.** *Proceedings of ACL 2024.* arXiv:2310.17976.
- Tu, Q., Fan, S., Tian, Z., & Yan, R. (2024). **CharacterEval: A Chinese Benchmark for Role-Playing Conversational Agent Evaluation.** *Proceedings of ACL 2024.* [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.638/).
- Li, K., Liu, T., Bashkansky, N., Bau, D., Viégas, F., Pfister, H., & Wattenberg, M. (2024). **Measuring and Controlling Instruction (In)Stability in Language Model Dialogs.** *COLM 2024.* arXiv:2402.10962. Documents persona drift over long dialogues, relevant to [character drift](character-development-vs-drift.md).
- Park, J. S., O'Brien, J. C., Cai, C. J., Morris, M. R., Liang, P., & Bernstein, M. S. (2023). **Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior.** *Proceedings of UIST 2023.* arXiv:2304.03442. Memory and reflection for believable characters.
- Bickmore, T. W., & Picard, R. W. (2005). **Establishing and Maintaining Long-Term Human-Computer Relationships.** *ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction, 12*(2), 293–327. [doi:10.1145/1067860.1067867](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1067860.1067867).
