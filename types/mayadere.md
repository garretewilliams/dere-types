# Mayadere

## What Is a Mayadere?

A mayadere is a Japanese-inspired character archetype who begins as an antagonist, rival or dangerous figure and becomes an ally or love interest after a turning point. The archetype is defined by that change of sides, and by the edge the character keeps from their earlier role.

## Quick Definition

**Mayadere:** A character archetype who begins as an antagonist, rival or dangerous figure and becomes an ally or love interest after a turning point, while often retaining an edge from their earlier role.

## Meaning

The *dere* element comes from *dere dere* (デレデレ), meaning lovey-dovey or infatuated. The origin of *maya* is unclear. Mayadere is a fan-coined term, and fandom sources give conflicting explanations for that element. This page does not endorse any of them.

*Etymology confidence: uncertain.* See [references](../docs/references.md#terminology-references).

## Defining Traits

- Initial hostility or danger
- Competence
- Independence
- Capacity for change
- A lingering edge

## Typical Behavior

- Opposes or threatens the protagonist early on.
- Changes allegiance after a turning point.
- Keeps traits from their antagonistic past: sharpness, ruthlessness or secrecy.
- May struggle with trust on both sides of the relationship.

## Relationship Behavior

The relationship changes after a turning point, often a defeat, a rescue, a shared enemy or a revelation. Affection develops from rivalry or conflict, and the earlier conflict remains part of the relationship's history rather than being erased.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"A mayadere stops being dangerous once they fall for someone."** Many keep their edge; it is simply redirected.
- **"Any reformed villain is a mayadere."** The term usually implies a relational turn, toward alliance or affection, not reform in general.
- **"The term has a well-documented etymology."** It does not.

## Mayadere in AI Character Design

The mayadere is defined by an arc, which makes it unusual among dere types: the character's correct behavior depends on *where they are in the story*. That makes memory essential.

| Behavioral dimension | Mayadere tendency |
|---|---|
| Dialogue style | Before the turn: hostile, guarded, cutting. After: sharp but loyal |
| Reaction to praise | Suspicion early; awkward acceptance later |
| Reaction to rejection or being ignored | Pride, coldness; possible reversion to old habits |
| Conflict | Direct and formidable |
| Affection | Protective, understated, sometimes grudging |
| Vulnerability | Linked to their past; guilt or fear of not being trusted |
| Relationship progression | Defined by the turning point and what follows |

**Design guidance**

- Track the story state. A post-turn mayadere who suddenly acts like a pre-turn antagonist (or the reverse) is a continuity failure, not characterization.
- Keep the edge. Turning sweet and harmless erases the archetype.
- Let the past matter: references to former conflict are part of what makes the relationship meaningful.

## Character Consistency

For a mayadere, consistency means behaving appropriately for the current point in the arc while remaining the same person. The turning point is [development](../docs/character-development-vs-drift.md) with a clear cause. Losing all memory of the antagonistic past, or flipping back without reason, is drift.
