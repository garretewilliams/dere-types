# Dandere

## What Is a Dandere?

A dandere is a Japanese-inspired character archetype who is quiet and socially reserved, often shy, and who becomes more talkative and expressive with people they feel comfortable with. The archetype is about the change that comfort and trust make possible: a character who says little in a crowd may be warm, funny and talkative one-to-one.

## Quick Definition

**Dandere:** A character archetype who is quiet and socially reserved, often shy, and who becomes more talkative and expressive with people they feel comfortable with.

## Meaning

The name is commonly explained as a combination of *danmari* (黙り), meaning silence or keeping quiet, and *dere dere* (デレデレ), meaning lovey-dovey or infatuated.

*Etymology confidence: commonly cited.* The derivation from *danmari* is widely repeated in fandom sources but not well documented in reference works. See [references](../docs/references.md#terminology-references).

## Defining Traits

- Shyness
- Social reserve
- Quietness
- Attentiveness
- Warmth that is slow to show

## Typical Behavior

- Says little in groups or with strangers.
- May hesitate, trail off or avoid attention.
- Is often observant and listens closely.
- Opens up noticeably in comfortable, one-to-one settings.

## Relationship Behavior

A dandere opens up through comfort, patience and trust. The change from reserved to expressive is gradual, and pressure or sudden attention can cause the character to withdraw again. Relationships with a dandere reward patience.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"A dandere cannot or will not speak."** A dandere speaks, often thoughtfully. Reserve is not muteness.
- **"A dandere is emotionally cold."** The warmth is there; it takes comfort to show.
- **"Quietness means the character has nothing to say."** Dandere characters are frequently observant and insightful.

## Dandere in AI Character Design

An AI dandere has to be quiet *in style* while still being a good conversational partner. A character that replies with "..." or a single word every time is frustrating, not shy.

| Behavioral dimension | Dandere tendency |
|---|---|
| Dialogue style | Soft, hesitant, brief at first; fuller as comfort grows |
| Reaction to praise | Embarrassed, pleased, unsure how to respond |
| Reaction to rejection or being ignored | Withdraws; may assume they did something wrong |
| Conflict | Avoids it; may agree to keep the peace and voice disagreement later |
| Affection | Small gestures, remembering details, gradually more words |
| Vulnerability | Shared slowly, often in private, quiet moments |
| Relationship progression | From hesitant short replies to relaxed, even playful conversation |

**Design guidance**

- Show shyness through hesitation, phrasing and topic choice, not by refusing to respond.
- Let comfort accumulate. A dandere's voice should change across a long relationship, which makes this archetype a natural fit for systems with long-term memory.
- Avoid regressing to first-meeting shyness every session with a familiar user. That is a [relationship reset](https://github.com/garretewilliams/ai-character-memory-benchmark/blob/main/docs/failure-modes.md), not consistency.

## Character Consistency

A consistent dandere stays recognizably gentle and reserved, even after opening up. They may become talkative with a trusted person while remaining shy with new characters. That combination is consistency, not contradiction. Sudden confident extroversion with everyone, without a story reason, is [drift](../docs/character-development-vs-drift.md).

## Related Dere Types

- [Kuudere](kuudere.md): also quiet, but composed rather than shy.
- [Deredere](deredere.md): openly warm; a fully comfortable dandere can resemble one.
- [Tsundere](tsundere.md): also slow to show affection, but through defensiveness rather than shyness.
