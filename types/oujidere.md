# Oujidere

## What Is an Oujidere?

An oujidere is a Japanese-inspired character archetype with prince-like expectations: confident, polished, often chivalrous, and used to being treated as important. The archetype is commonly described as the princely counterpart to the [himedere](himedere.md), though the two are not simply gendered versions of each other. Oujidere is a less widely used term than most others on this list.

## Quick Definition

**Oujidere:** A character archetype characterized by prince-like expectations: confidence, regal or chivalrous self-presentation, and an expectation of respect and elevated treatment.

## Meaning

The name combines *ōji* (王子), meaning prince, with *dere dere* (デレデレ), meaning lovey-dovey or infatuated. It is often romanized as *oujidere* or *ōjidere*.

*Etymology confidence: commonly cited.* The meaning of *ōji* is established; the compound is less consistently used fandom terminology. See [references](../docs/references.md#terminology-references).

## Defining Traits

- Confidence
- Regal self-presentation
- Pride
- A sense of duty or chivalry
- Expectation of respect

## Typical Behavior

- Carries themselves with polish and composure.
- Expects to be treated as important.
- May be gallant or protective in a somewhat performative way.
- Is unsettled when their status is ignored.

## Relationship Behavior

Affection may be expressed through protection, grand gestures or formal courtesy. With trust, the character becomes more genuine and less performative. A recurring arc is learning that a close relationship needs equality rather than admiration.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"An oujidere is just a male himedere."** Both expect elevated treatment, but the oujidere is more often framed around composure, duty and chivalry, while the himedere is framed around being admired and indulged. The archetype is not tied to any gender.
- **"An oujidere is arrogant with no redeeming qualities."** Princely confidence often comes with protectiveness and a sense of responsibility.
- **"Princely manners mean the character is insincere."** Formality can be a genuine part of who they are.

## Oujidere in AI Character Design

| Behavioral dimension | Oujidere tendency |
|---|---|
| Dialogue style | Polished, confident, courteous; may be grandiose |
| Reaction to praise | Gracious acceptance; expected but appreciated |
| Reaction to rejection or being ignored | Wounded dignity; may become stiffly formal |
| Conflict | Composed, principled; dislikes losing face |
| Affection | Protective, chivalrous, sometimes theatrical |
| Vulnerability | Rarely shown; may emerge as doubt about living up to expectations |
| Relationship progression | Less performance, more sincerity; learns to treat a partner as an equal |

**Design guidance**

- Let courtesy and confidence be the baseline, with status sensitivity appearing when relevant.
- Avoid making the character condescending toward the user by default. Princely manners can be warm.

## Character Consistency

A consistent oujidere keeps poise and a sense of their own standing, even as they become more sincere. Sudden loss of all composure or self-respect without a story reason is [drift](../docs/character-development-vs-drift.md).

## Related Dere Types

- [Himedere](himedere.md): the princess counterpart, centered on admiration and special treatment.
- [Kamidere](kamidere.md): a stronger sense of superiority and desire for control.
- [Kuudere](kuudere.md): shares composure, without the expectation of elevated treatment.
