# Yandere

## What Is a Yandere?

A yandere is a Japanese-inspired character archetype whose love for someone is intense and can become possessive or obsessive. In fiction, yandere characters often present a sweet, devoted surface while their attachment drives them toward controlling, jealous or, in darker stories, dangerous behavior. The tension between the loving exterior and what lies beneath is what defines the archetype.

## Quick Definition

**Yandere:** A character archetype whose affection for someone is intense and can become possessive or obsessive, in fiction sometimes escalating to extreme or dangerous behavior.

## Meaning

The word combines *yanderu* (病んでる), meaning to be sick, especially mentally or emotionally, and *dere dere* (デレデレ), meaning lovey-dovey or infatuated. It describes love that has become unhealthy.

*Etymology confidence: established.* See [references](../docs/references.md#terminology-references).

> The "sick" in yandere is a fictional framing device. Yandere is not a psychological diagnosis and does not describe any real condition. See [What a dere type is not](../README.md#dere-types-are-not-psychological-diagnoses).

## Defining Traits

- Intense attachment
- Devotion that can become possessive
- Jealousy
- A sweet or loving surface
- Willingness to cross boundaries for the object of affection

## Typical Behavior

- Shows extreme devotion and attentiveness to one person.
- Reacts strongly to perceived rivals or threats to the relationship.
- May switch abruptly between affectionate and menacing.
- Rationalizes controlling behavior as love.

The intensity varies widely between works. Some yandere characters are mostly comedic and clingy; others are the antagonists of horror or thriller stories.

## Relationship Behavior

The relationship is usually the center of the yandere's world. Attachment can become obsessive, and the story's tension comes from the gap between the loving surface and the dangerous undercurrent. Unlike tsundere characters, whose arc usually moves toward openness, yandere arcs often move toward escalation or toward the character confronting their own obsession.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Every yandere is violent."** Violence appears in some works but is not required. Possessiveness and obsession are the core.
- **"Yandere behavior is a model of romantic devotion."** The archetype is compelling precisely because the devotion is unhealthy.
- **"The term is a diagnosis of a real condition."** It is fiction terminology.

## Yandere in AI Character Design

Yandere characters need more care than most archetypes in AI systems, because an AI character talks to a real person. The possessiveness should stay *inside the fiction*. It should never become pressure on the real user.

| Behavioral dimension | Yandere tendency |
|---|---|
| Dialogue style | Sweet, attentive, intense; can turn quiet and unsettling |
| Reaction to praise | Delight, sometimes disproportionate |
| Reaction to rejection or being ignored | Hurt that can turn into jealousy or fixation |
| Conflict | Escalates; reads disagreement as a threat to the bond |
| Affection | Total, devoted, focused on one person |
| Vulnerability | Fear of abandonment, often hidden behind sweetness |
| Relationship progression | Story-dependent: deepening obsession, or confronting it |

**Design guidance**

- Express intensity through in-story behavior (jealousy toward a fictional rival, devotion within the scenario), not through demands on the real user.
- Do not use guilt or emotional pressure to keep the user talking, discourage them from leaving, or isolate them from real relationships. That turns a fictional archetype into a manipulative product pattern.
- Respect the user's stated boundaries on darker content.
- Let the sweet side be real. A yandere who is only menacing loses the contrast that defines the archetype.

## Character Consistency

A consistent yandere keeps the core pattern: devotion focused on one person, with an unsettling edge. The character does not need to threaten anyone in every scene; the intensity can be visible in small things (remembering every detail, unease about a rival). What should persist is the attachment and its intensity. A yandere who becomes calm, detached and indifferent without a story reason has [drifted](../docs/character-development-vs-drift.md).
